Staff Investigative Report 
June 26, 2026 
I. Introduction 
This report presents the findings of the Metropolitan Human Relations Commission ("MHRC") 
staff following an investigation into allegations concerning the administration of federally-
funded housing assistance and housing stabilization services by the Metropolitan Government of 
Nashville and Davidson County Office of Homeless Services ("OHS"). The investigation was 
conducted pursuant to the Commission's authority under the Metropolitan Charter, the 
Metropolitan Code of Laws, the Commission's Rules and Procedures, and applicable federal civil 
rights laws governing recipients of federal financial assistance. 
The investigation concerns allegations that OHS, in administering bridge housing placements, 
rental assistance, housing navigation services, and access to permanent housing resources, may 
have employed inconsistent or inadequately-defined policies and practices that resulted in 
unequal treatment or a disparate impact on protected classes, particularly Black women and 
women of color experiencing homelessness. 
The allegations arise from complaints submitted to the Commission, documentary evidence 
obtained during the investigation, interviews with complainants, current and former service 
providers, former OHS personnel, and other witnesses possessing relevant information regarding 
the administration of these programs. 
The purpose of this report is not to determine ultimate liability or make findings equivalent to 
those rendered by a court of law. Rather, consistent with Rule 2.7 of the MHRC Rules and 
Procedures, this report evaluates whether sufficient evidence exists to establish probable cause to 
believe that an unlawful discriminatory practice may have occurred, thereby warranting further 
proceedings, including conciliation. 
II. Jurisdiction 
The Metropolitan Human Relations Commission is authorized to investigate allegations of 
unlawful discrimination within its jurisdiction, including discrimination arising from the 
administration of federally-assisted programs and activities. 
At all times relevant to this investigation, the Office of Homeless Services administered or 
coordinated programs supported through federal financial assistance, including funding 
authorized under the American Rescue Plan Act (ARPA), U.S. Department of Housing and 
Urban Development (HUD) programs, Continuum of Care (CoC) funding, Emergency Solutions 
Grant (ESG) funding, and other federal and local housing initiatives. OHS exercised substantial 
authority over participant selection, housing placement, funding allocation, housing navigation, 
rental assistance administration, and coordination with nonprofit service providers participating 
in federally-funded homelessness response systems. 
1 
 
Because these programs were administered with federal financial assistance, they are subject to 
the nondiscrimination requirements of Title VI of the Civil Rights Act of 1964 and its 
implementing regulations, which prohibit discrimination on the basis of race, color, or national 
origin in programs receiving federal financial assistance, as well as state and local law and 
policy. 
Accordingly, MHRC possesses jurisdiction to investigate whether the administration of these 
federally assisted housing programs complied with applicable civil rights requirements.

IV. Allegations 
The investigation was conducted to determine whether the evidence supports the following 
findings: 
1. Whether OHS administered federally funded housing programs pursuant to written policies, 
objective eligibility criteria, and consistently applied procedures governing participant selection, 
rental assistance, and housing transitions. 
2 
 
2. Whether participants for bridge housing, rental assistance, and housing navigation services 
were selected using transparent, documented, and consistently applied criteria, including whether 
Coordinated Entry tools or standardized prioritization systems were uniformly utilized. 
3. Whether participants received accurate, consistent, and complete information regarding 
duration of rental assistance, eligibility for housing vouchers, and pathways to permanent 
housing. 
4. Whether modifications to rental assistance or participant financial obligations were 
implemented pursuant to established policies, and whether such changes were consistently 
communicated and documented. 
5. Whether OHS maintained adequate documentation supporting participant selection, 
prioritization decisions, voucher referrals, program exits, and rental assistance determinations. 
6. Whether similarly-situated participants received different treatment with respect to housing 
placement, rental assistance, voucher access, or program continuation without documented 
justification. 
7. Whether facially-neutral policies or practices governing shelter exits, housing placement, and 
rental assistance had a disproportionate adverse impact on protected classes, including Black 
women and women of color experiencing homelessness. 
8. Whether the totality of the evidence supports a reasonable basis to believe that the 
administration of federally funded housing programs by OHS may have violated Title VI or 
related nondiscrimination requirements. 
V. Standard of Review 
Pursuant to Rule 2.7 of the MHRC Rules and Procedures, the purpose of an investigation is to 
determine whether probable cause exists to believe that an unlawful discriminatory practice may 
have occurred. 
Probable cause is a threshold determination. It does not require proof by a preponderance of the 
evidence or proof beyond a reasonable doubt. Rather, the inquiry is whether the evidence, 
viewed as a whole, provides a reasonable basis to believe that unlawful discrimination may have 
occurred and that further proceedings are warranted. 
Where evidence was conflicting, staff evaluated credibility based upon corroboration, 
consistency, documentary support, contemporaneous records, and the plausibility of each 
account when considered in context of the entire investigative record. 
No adverse inference was drawn solely from disputed testimony where documentary evidence 
remained incomplete or unavailable.

VIII. Background Information 
The Office of Homeless Services ("OHS") serves as Metropolitan Nashville and Davidson 
County's lead agency for coordinating the local response to homelessness. During the period 
relevant to this investigation, OHS administered, coordinated, or oversaw numerous housing and 
homelessness initiatives funded through a combination of federal, state, local, and private 
resources. 
Among its responsibilities, OHS coordinated emergency shelter operations, housing navigation, 
landlord engagement, rental assistance, supportive services, homeless management information 
systems, and the administration of federal grants intended to increase access to stable housing for 
individuals and families experiencing homelessness. 
OHS also served as a central coordinating entity among nonprofit service providers, property 
owners, housing developers, and governmental partners participating in Nashville's homelessness 
response system. 
Because many of these activities were supported through federal financial assistance, OHS was 
required to administer its programs in accordance with applicable federal civil rights 
requirements, including Title VI of the Civil Rights Act of 1964. 
Nashville participates in HUD's Continuum of Care ("CoC") program, which is intended to 
create a coordinated system for addressing homelessness through community-wide planning, 
prioritization, and allocation of housing resources. 
As part of the CoC framework, participating agencies utilize a Coordinated Entry process 
designed to assess individuals and families experiencing homelessness, evaluate vulnerability 
and housing needs, and prioritize participants for available housing resources. 
The Coordinated Entry system is intended to promote consistency, transparency, and equitable 
access by reducing reliance upon informal referrals or subjective decision-making. Under this 
framework, participants are generally assessed using standardized tools and referred to available 
4 
 
housing resources according to established prioritization criteria, while recognizing that certain 
funding streams or specialized programs may have additional eligibility requirements. 
The effectiveness of Coordinated Entry depends upon the consistent application of objective 
criteria and accurate documentation of participant assessments, referrals, and program status1. 
The demographic composition of the population served by Nashville's homelessness response 
system provides important context for evaluating the potential impact of administrative 
decisions. 
Nationally, Black individuals and families are disproportionately represented among people 
experiencing homelessness despite comprising a substantially smaller percentage of the overall 
population2. Research has consistently shown that Black families, particularly Black women with 
children, are overrepresented among households utilizing emergency shelters and family shelter 
systems3. 
Available national outreach data and point-in-time count similarly indicate that families residing 
in Nashville's emergency shelters include a disproportionately high percentage of Black residents 
and other people of color4. By contrast, outreach data and point-in-time counts have often shown 
a comparatively higher proportion of unsheltered individuals living in encampments who 
identify as White, although demographic patterns vary over time and by location. 
These demographic realities do not establish discrimination. They do, however, underscore why 
decisions affecting access to shelter exits, bridge housing, rental assistance, and permanent 
housing opportunities warrant careful examination under Title VI when the affected participant 
population is disproportionately composed of members of protected classes.

housing placement, rental assistance, and housing navigation services.

decreased.  
14. Participants were subsequently notified of increased rent obligations during 2025.  
15. Several participants reported receiving lease violation notices, eviction notices, or rent 
demands following reductions in rental assistance.  
16. Participants described the changes in rent obligations as destabilizing and inconsistent with 
their understanding of the terms under which they entered the program.

17. Two of the eight participants did not obtain housing vouchers.  
18. The investigation has not yet received complete documentation identifying the written 
policies governing participant selection, rental assistance duration, or program exit criteria for 
the initiative.  
19. Multiple witnesses described variations in how policies were communicated and applied 
across participants and providers.  
20. The record reflects gaps in documentation regarding how individual participant decisions 
were made, including selection and prioritization decisions.

25. Participants affected by the program at issue include a substantial proportion of Black

X. Findings on Allegations and Probable Cause Determination 
Based on the Findings of Fact set forth in Section IX, MHRC staff makes the following 
determinations as to each allegation: 
1.  Whether OHS administered federally-funded housing programs pursuant to written 
policies, objective eligibility criteria, and consistently applied procedures governing 
participant selection, rental assistance, and housing transitions.

The investigation finds that OHS has not produced complete written policies governing 
participant selection, rental assistance duration, or housing transition criteria for the initiative at 
issue.   
2.  Whether participants for bridge housing, rental assistance, and housing navigation 
services were selected using transparent, documented, and consistently applied criteria, 
including whether Coordinated Entry tools or standardized prioritization systems were 
uniformly utilized. 
The investigation finds that participants were selected from emergency shelter populations, 
including overflow populations from the Nashville Rescue Mission.  The record does not 
establish that standardized Coordinated Entry tools or documented prioritization criteria were 
uniformly applied to all participants. 
3.  Whether participants received accurate, consistent, and complete information regarding: 
duration of rental assistance, eligibility for housing vouchers, and pathways to 
permanent housing. 
The investigation finds the conflicting accounts regarding the information provided to 
participants concerning. 
4.  Whether modifications to rental assistance or participant financial obligations were 
implemented pursuant to established policies, and whether such changes were 
consistently communicated and documented. 
The investigation finds that participants experienced reductions in rental assistance obligations 
and/or increases in tenant responsibility during 2025. Witness testimony indicates these changes 
may have been implemented in response to funding concerns and were not consistently 
communicated across all participants. 
5.  Whether OHS maintained adequate documentation supporting: participant selection, 
prioritization decisions, voucher referrals, program exits, and rental assistance 
determinations. 
The investigation finds that OHS has failed to produce any written documentation nor responded 
to the multiple attempts to obtain answers to these questions. 
6.  Whether similarly situated participants received different treatment with respect to 
housing placement, rental assistance, voucher access, or program continuation without 
documented justification. 
The investigation finds that similarly situated participants experienced different outcomes with 
respect to rental assistance adjustments and housing transition outcomes. 
8 
 
7.  Whether facially neutral policies or practices governing shelter exits, housing placement, 
and rental assistance had a disproportionate adverse impact on protected classes, 
including Black women and women of color experiencing homelessness. 
The investigation finds that the affected population includes a substantial proportion of Black 
women and women of color, shelter-based housing interventions disproportionately affect these 
populations nationally; and the challenged practices govern access to shelter exits, housing 
stability, and the 8 women and their children affected by OHS’s practices were 
disproportionately Black and women of color.  
8.  Whether the totality of the evidence supports a reasonable basis to believe that the 
administration of federally funded housing programs by OHS may have violated Title VI 
or related nondiscrimination requirements. 
Based on the totality of the evidence, the investigation finds that there is a probable cause to 
believe that OHS’s administration of federally-funded housing programs did not comply with 
Title VI requirements.
